## Hello!{.post-header}

I'm Ian Clester. Computers are fun (*now more than ever!*), and I'm broadly interested in **lively computation**: programs and processes you can play with. My research at [Georgia Tech](https://gtcmt.gatech.edu/), where I obtained a [PhD in Music Technology](https://music.gatech.edu/phd-music-technology) (advised by [Jason Freeman](https://distributedmusic.gatech.edu/jason/)), focused on **computational music** --- music which is represented as a program rather than a fixed score or recording --- and its implications. (This idea is related to [process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_music), [aleatoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleatoric_music), and [generative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_music) music.) Other interests include HCI and programming languages.

These days, I'm putting these ideas into practice at [NoneType](https://nonety.pe/) with Ash Vinay, where we're building **Tilde**: a collaborative, flexible, and *fun* environment for working with sound and computation.

Before Georgia Tech, I did a Master's in [EECS](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/) at MIT (in [Signal Kinetics](https://www.media.mit.edu/groups/signal-kinetics/overview/)), and before that my undergrad in EECS and [Music](https://mta.mit.edu/music). Along the way, I interned at various places, including [Harmonix](https://www.harmonixmusic.com/), [Google](https://www.google.com/), and [Astranis](https://www.astranis.com/), and did a little work for the [Folk](https://folk.computer/) folks.
Before all that, I thought computers were cool and music was magic. And I still do.

Here you can find information about my [research]({{ site.baseurl }}/research/) and [personal projects]({{ site.baseurl }}/projects/), plus [contact info]({{ site.baseurl }}/contact/), and even a few [blog posts]({{ site.baseurl }}/posts/). You can also find some of my software that runs in the browser, such as [kilobeat]({{ site.baseurl }}/kilobeat/), [Alternator]({{ site.baseurl }}/alternator/), and [ScoreCard]({{ site.baseurl }}/s).
